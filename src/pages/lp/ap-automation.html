Accounts payable automation, defined

What is an AP agent, and how is it different from AP automation?

An AP agent is AI software that runs the accounts payable process end to end. It takes in supplier invoices, extracts the data, verifies the vendor, matches each invoice to the purchase order and goods receipt, checks contract pricing and company policy, decides what to do, explains the decision, and posts the result to the ERP. People handle only the exceptions it routes to them.

AP automation (accounts payable automation) is the broader practice of replacing manual accounts payable steps with software: keying invoices, checking for duplicates, matching, GL coding, chasing approvals, scheduling payments and posting. Traditional AP automation software moves invoices through fixed workflow steps and leaves the judgement calls to people; an AP agent reads the surrounding context and makes the call, asking a person only when its confidence is below the threshold you set.

Invoice fraud detection

Invoice fraud detection in AP automation: eight checks before a dollar moves.

Most invoice fraud is quiet: a bank account changed by email, an invoice sent twice, a total nudged up, a vendor that was never real. The AP agent runs these checks on every invoice, and an invoice that fails any of them is held before it reaches the ERP.

Duplicate and double billing

Exact and fuzzy matching against paid history: same vendor, amount and date with a different invoice number still gets caught, as does the same invoice resent through a different channel.

Bank-detail change hold

A new account or remit-to address freezes payment until it is verified against the number already on the vendor file, never against a number in the email that asked for the change.

Vendor authentication

Legal name, tax ID, address and bank account are checked against the vendor master and the W-9 on file; new vendors are screened against sanctions and watch lists before their first payment.

Sender and domain check

The address an invoice arrives from is compared with the vendor's known domains. Look-alike domains and free-mail senders for established vendors are flagged.

Document tampering

PDF metadata, fonts and edit history are examined for signs the file was altered after it was issued, such as a changed total or bank line pasted over the original.

Atypical amount detection

Each invoice is compared with the vendor's own history: amount, frequency, line mix and unit prices. An invoice that sits outside the pattern is routed for review with the comparison shown.

Match and contract evidence

Nothing posts without a PO, receipt or contract clause that explains it. Round-number invoices with no PO, or quantities above what was received, are held.

Segregation of duties

The person who creates or changes a vendor cannot approve its invoices or release its payments. Every approval and release is logged with who, when and why.

Rules

How does the AP agent learn your AP policy?

You don't rebuild your policy as workflow diagrams. The documents your controller already keeps, the AP policy, supplier terms and the approval matrix, are read the same way invoices are, and each clause becomes a rule in the Modify Rules screen with the sentence it came from attached.

Where do the rules come from?
From your own documents. The agent proposes a rule per clause; nothing is active until someone on your team switches it on.
What happens before a rule goes live?
It is run against your last 30 days of invoices so you can see exactly which ones it would have posted, routed or held, before it touches a live one.
Who maintains them?
Finance, in plain language. Change a tolerance or an approver and the next invoice follows the new rule; the old version stays in the audit trail.

Learning

Does the AP agent get more accurate over time?

Yes, and in a way you can see. The agent only asks a person when its confidence is below the threshold you set. Each answer is stored as a named rule with the person who gave it, so the same question is never asked twice and the exception rate keeps falling.

What decides whether a person is asked?
A confidence score on every decision. Above your threshold the invoice posts; below it the agent stops and shows the evidence. It does not guess.
What happens to the answer?
It becomes a rule in the same screen as your policy rules, with the invoice it came from and the colleague who answered. You can edit or retire it like any other.
What does that look like after two months?
In a typical rollout, exceptions fall from around 40 per 100 invoices in week one to single digits by week eight, with the remaining ones concentrated in new vendors and contract changes.
