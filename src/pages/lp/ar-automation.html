Definition

What is accounts receivable (AR) automation?

Accounts receivable (AR) automation is software that handles the work between sending an invoice and closing it in the ledger: requesting payment, capturing remittance advice, matching each payment to the right open invoices, coding short pays and deductions, posting the cash receipt to the ERP and following up on anything still outstanding. Star Software does this with document-reading AI agents that already process mill test reports, certificates of analysis and supplier invoices for industrial companies — so the same engine that reads a heat number on an MTR reads an invoice number on a remittance. AR automation is also called A/R automation, receivables automation or, when it extends to order entry and invoicing, order-to-cash (O2C) automation.

Why it matters

Cash sits unapplied for days

Manual cash application averages three to seven days from deposit to posting. Until then, credit holds block shipments to customers who have already paid.

Why it matters

Deductions hide in the noise

Weight variances, freight allowances, scrap credits and rebates arrive as short pays with no explanation. Catching them after posting means chasing pennies months later.

Why it matters

Collectors chase, they don't collect

Without a live view of promises, disputes and portal activity, AR teams send reminders to customers who have already paid and miss the ones who haven't.

Deductions & Short-pay handling: coded before posting, routed to the right owner

Industrial customers short-pay for reasons that are legitimate but undocumented: a 1.8% weight variance on a coil, a freight allowance agreed by email, a scrap return, a price-in-effect adjustment, a rebate accrual. Star compares every payment to the invoice, classifies the difference against your deduction policy and historical patterns, writes off what is within tolerance, and routes the rest to the person who can decide — with the invoice, the remittance and the customer thread attached.

  • Tolerance rules from your SOP — "under $250 or 0.5%, whichever is lower, write off to tolerance account" becomes an executable rule.
  • Industry deduction codes — weight/theoretical variance, freight, scrap credit, quality claim, price adjustment, early-pay discount, bank fee, tax rounding, rebate.
  • Dispute routing — to sales, quality or logistics in Teams, Slack or email; resolution closes the item in the ERP.
  • Overpayments handled — applied as credit or queued for refund according to policy.
  • Learning loop — every human decision becomes a rule, so the same deduction never reaches a person twice.

How does accounts receivable automation work for a steel service center?

A service center invoices by actual weight, ships against blanket POs and sells to fabricators and OEMs who short-pay for weight variances, freight and scrap returns. Star reads the remittance, matches it against the open items in Stratix, eSTELPLAN or INVEX, compares invoiced weight to the customer's scale ticket when it is attached, and codes the difference as a weight variance, freight allowance or quality claim — before the receipt posts. Payment requests go out with the invoice, the packing list and the MTR for the heat, so customers can pay without calling to ask for a cert.

Where the time goes today

  • Keying lockbox and ACH receipts into the metals ERP, then hunting for the invoice numbers the customer left off.
  • Reconciling theoretical vs. actual weight disputes months after the coil shipped.
  • Credit holds on good customers whose cheque is sitting in the lockbox queue.
  • Customers asking for the MTR before they will release payment.

Built for how service centers price and invoice

Service centers (service centres in Canada) price coil, sheet, plate and bar per CWT or per piece, invoice on theoretical or actual scale weight, add alloy, fuel and energy surcharges, and bill processing — slitting, cut-to-length, shearing, sawing, toll processing — as separate lines. Star reads every remittance against those lines, so a short pay on a surcharge, a drop or remnant credit, or a theoretical-versus-actual weight difference is identified and coded instead of disappearing into unapplied cash.

Code them before the cash posts. Star Software checks each short pay against the surcharge and price-in-effect on the invoice, writes off amounts within tolerance and routes the rest to inside sales with the invoice, remittance and customer email attached.

Built for wholesaler chargebacks and gross-to-net

Pharmaceutical manufacturers are paid mainly by the big three wholesalers — McKesson, Cencora and Cardinal Health — net of chargebacks, rebates, GPO administration fees, returns and service fees. Star connects each remittance to the EDI 844 chargeback request and EDI 849 response, validates every line against WAC and contract price, NDC and eligibility, applies valid deductions, disputes invalid ones within the deadline and posts the net receipt with lot traceability, closing gross-to-net leakage that generic accounts receivable tools miss.

Questions pharmaceutical manufacturers ask

How do pharma manufacturers reconcile wholesaler chargebacks with payments?

Match each wholesaler remittance to the chargeback lines it deducts, then validate every line against contract pricing before applying it. Star Software reads the remittance and EDI 844/849 data together, applies valid chargebacks, disputes invalid ones and posts the net receipt to SAP or NetSuite.

What is the difference between EDI 844 and EDI 849?

EDI 844 is the product transfer account adjustment a wholesaler sends to claim a chargeback; EDI 849 is the manufacturer's response accepting or rejecting each line. Star Software uses both to validate the deductions that appear on wholesaler payments.

How does AI reduce gross-to-net leakage from invalid deductions?

By checking every deduction line against contract price, eligibility and duplicate claims before the cash is applied, and disputing what fails inside the claim window. Star Software flags invalid lines automatically, so recoveries that are usually written off are captured.

What can Star's AI agents help with?

Star Software's AI agents do more than data entry. They read business documents, verify them against your ERP and source records, analyse them against each customer's or supplier's past history, and make decisions — applying cash, coding deductions, flagging non-conforming material, releasing credit holds — inside the rules and approval limits you set. 01

Read

Extract every field from any layout — email, PDF, scan, spreadsheet, EDI — with no templates. In AR: payer, amount, invoice references and handwritten notes from a cheque stub.

02

Verify

Glossary

Accounts receivable terms, defined

AR automation
Software that automates the work between issuing an invoice and closing it: payment requests, remittance capture, cash application, deductions, collections and ERP posting.
Cash application
Matching each customer payment to the open invoices it pays and recording that application in the ledger.
Remittance advice
The document or data a customer sends to explain which invoices a payment covers and any amounts deducted.
Payment request
A secure pay-now link sent with one or more invoices or a statement, letting the customer pay by ACH or card without logging in.
Short pay
A payment for less than the invoiced amount, usually because the customer has taken a deduction.
Deduction
An amount a customer withholds from payment for a reason such as weight variance, freight, scrap return, pricing, rebate or a quality claim.
Weight variance
The difference between invoiced (often theoretical) weight and the weight the customer received, a common cause of short pays in metals.
Unapplied cash
Money received but not yet matched to specific invoices, which distorts aging, DSO and credit decisions.
DSO (days sales outstanding)
The average number of days it takes to collect payment after a sale; lower DSO means faster cash.
Lockbox
A bank service that receives customer cheques, deposits them and sends images and data files to the supplier.
EDI 820
The electronic data interchange transaction for payment order / remittance advice, used by large customers to describe what they paid.
Straight-through processing
The share of payments applied and posted automatically, without a person touching them.
Order-to-cash (O2C)
The full cycle from customer order and credit check through invoicing, payment and cash posting; AR automation covers the stages after the invoice.
Cash posting
Recording a customer receipt in the general ledger; often used interchangeably with cash application.
Auto-match (hit) rate
The percentage of payments matched to invoices automatically, without a person reviewing them.
Theoretical weight
Steel weight calculated from dimensions and grade rather than measured on a scale; differences from actual weight cause short pays.
CWT pricing
Pricing per hundredweight (100 lb), common for steel coil, sheet, plate and bar sold by service centers.
Retainage
A percentage of an invoice the customer withholds until a project milestone or completion, common in metal fabrication and manufacturing.
Chargeback (pharma)
The difference between a wholesaler's acquisition cost and the lower contract price a manufacturer agreed with a customer, claimed back from the manufacturer.
EDI 844 / EDI 849
EDI 844 is a wholesaler's chargeback claim (product transfer account adjustment); EDI 849 is the manufacturer's response accepting or rejecting each line.
Gross-to-net
The reduction from a pharmaceutical product's list price to net revenue after chargebacks, rebates, fees and returns.
GPO
Group purchasing organization: a buying group that negotiates contract prices with manufacturers on behalf of members.

Accounts receivable automation basics

What is accounts receivable automation and how does it work?

Accounts receivable automation is software that handles the steps between sending an invoice and closing it in the ledger. It sends payment requests, captures remittances, applies cash to invoices, codes short pays and deductions, runs collection reminders and posts everything to the ERP. Star Software does this with AI agents. They read each document, verify it against your ERP and bank data, check it against customer history, and then apply the payment or route it to a person if it falls outside your rules.

What is the difference between accounts receivable automation and order-to-cash automation?

Order-to-cash (O2C) automation covers the whole cycle from order entry and credit checks through invoicing, payment and cash posting; accounts receivable automation is the part after the invoice — payment requests, cash application, collections and deductions. Star Software automates the AR stages and, through its order processing agent, the order-entry step as well.

How much can accounts receivable automation reduce DSO?

How much DSO drops depends on where your delays are. The biggest gains usually come from three places. First, payment requests sent at invoicing instead of waiting for the customer's AP cycle. Second, same-day cash application, so paid customers stop appearing past due. Third, resolving deductions while they are fresh. Star Software benchmarks your current cycle from sample documents before you commit.

Does AR automation mean accounts receivable automation?

Yes. In finance, AR stands for accounts receivable, the money customers owe a business. AR automation means automating receivables work such as cash application, collections, payment requests and deduction management. It has nothing to do with augmented reality. Star Software's AR automation is built for metals, manufacturing, pharmaceutical and wholesale distribution companies.

More about Star AR automation

What is cash application and why automate it?

Cash application is the process of matching incoming customer payments to the open invoices they settle and recording that application in the ledger. Automating it removes the manual lookup and keying that delays posting by days, eliminates unapplied cash at month-end, and surfaces short pays and deductions before they post rather than weeks later.

What is a payment request in Star?

A payment request is an emailed, secure pay-now link tied to one or more open invoices or a statement. The customer selects what to pay, adds remittance notes and pays by ACH or card without creating an account. Because the invoice references travel with the payment, the receipt posts straight through to the ERP.

Which remittance formats can Star read?

Email bodies and attachments (PDF, Excel, Word, images), EDI 820 and 844 files, ACH CTX addenda, BAI2 and MT940 bank statements, lockbox and cheque images, and exports from customer AP portals. Star's document AI already reads MTRs, COAs and invoices at scale, so unusual layouts are not a blocker.

What happens when an invoice number is missing or wrong on the remittance?

Star matches on amount, purchase-order number, customer reference, dates and historical patterns, proposes the most likely invoices with a confidence score, and either posts (above your threshold) or sends the proposed match to a reviewer. The reviewer's answer is remembered as a rule for that customer.

How does Star handle short pays and deductions in metals?

Each payment is compared to the invoice. Differences are classified against your deduction policy — weight or theoretical variance, freight allowance, scrap credit, price-in-effect adjustment, quality claim, rebate, bank fee — and either written off within tolerance or routed to the owner in sales, quality or logistics with the evidence attached. The invoice stays open until the credit memo or dispute resolution closes it.
