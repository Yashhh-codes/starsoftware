Definition

What is cash application?

Cash application is the accounts receivable process of matching each incoming customer payment to the open invoices it pays and recording that application in the general ledger, so invoices close, aging is accurate and credit limits reflect reality. Automated cash application uses software to capture the remittance detail, propose or make the match, handle differences such as short pays, deductions and overpayments, and post the cash receipt to the ERP without manual keying. Cash application is also called cash posting, payment application, payment matching, remittance matching or auto cash.

Why is manual cash application so slow?

Because the payment and the explanation of the payment rarely arrive together. The money lands in the bank; the remittance arrives separately as an email, a PDF, a portal download, a cheque stub or an EDI file — often with truncated invoice numbers, combined payments across many invoices and unexplained differences. AR staff spend their day reconciling the two by hand, and the average deposit takes three to seven days to post.

What does it cost to leave it manual?

Unapplied cash distorts aging and DSO, credit holds block shipments to customers who have already paid, collectors chase invoices that are settled, and short pays discovered weeks after posting are hard to recover. For industrial companies selling by weight, by release or under contract pricing, those short pays are a meaningful share of margin.

How it works

How does automated cash application work?

Six steps from deposit to closed invoice. Each runs on its own rules and confidence thresholds, and every action is logged with the source document behind it.

Step 1 — Capture remittances from every channel

Star monitors the AR mailbox, SFTP folders, lockbox deliveries, bank statement files and customer AP portals, and pulls every remittance into a single queue the moment it arrives. Payments and their remittances are paired automatically even when they arrive days apart through different channels.

  • Shared AR inbox and forwarded emails, including remittance text written in the email body.
  • EDI 820 and ACH CTX / CCD+ addenda received through your bank or VAN.
  • Lockbox image files and cheque stubs, BAI2 and MT940 bank statements.
  • Scheduled downloads from customer portals that publish remittances.

Step 2 — Extract every field with document AI

The same engine that reads heat numbers and chemistry from mill test reports reads payer, payment amount, cheque or trace number, invoice references, discounts taken and deduction notes from any layout — typed, scanned, spreadsheet or free-text email. No per-customer templates to build or maintain.

  • Field-level confidence on every value, so low-confidence reads go to review instead of posting.
  • Line-level detail for remittances that list dozens or hundreds of invoices.
  • Handwritten notes on cheque stubs and marked-up statements are captured as remittance notes.

Step 3 — Match payments to open invoices

Star pulls open items from your ERP and matches each payment one-to-one, one-to-many or partially. When invoice numbers are missing, truncated or formatted differently, it matches on amount combinations, PO number, customer reference, dates and that customer's payment history — and attaches a confidence score to every proposed match.

  • Above your threshold, the match is applied automatically.
  • Below it, a reviewer sees the proposed match pre-filled and approves in one click.
  • Parent–child and multi-entity customers are handled without manual splitting.

Step 4 — Code short pays, deductions and overpayments before posting

Every difference between invoice and payment is classified against your deduction policy and history: weight variance, freight, scrap credit, price adjustment, quality claim, rebate, early-pay discount, bank fee or tax rounding. Small differences inside tolerance are written off to the right account; the rest are routed to the owner with the evidence attached.

  • Tolerance rules taken straight from your written policy.
  • Owner routing to sales, quality, logistics or credit in Teams, Slack or email.
  • Overpayments applied as on-account credit or queued for refund.

Step 5 — Post the cash receipt to the ERP

Star writes the cash receipt, the invoice applications, deduction and write-off entries and any on-account amounts to your ERP through its API or file import, with the batch reference and a link back to the source remittance. Invoices close, aging updates and credit holds release in the same minute.

  • Metals ERPs: Stratix, eSTELPLAN, INVEX, Metalware.
  • Mainstream ERPs: SAP, NetSuite, Dynamics 365, Epicor, Infor, Oracle, Sage, QuickBooks.
  • Optional remittance confirmation emailed to the customer from your AR address.

Step 6 — Review exceptions, and turn every answer into a rule

Exceptions arrive with the proposed match, the confidence score and the reason it was held. When a reviewer resolves one — "this customer always nets freight on the last invoice" — Star stores the decision as a rule for that customer, so the same exception does not come back. Straight-through rates rise week over week without anyone writing configuration.

  • Review queue in Star, or in Teams and Slack with approve / edit / reject actions.
  • Every rule traceable to the decision or policy clause that created it.
  • Confidence thresholds adjustable per customer, per entity or per amount.

Matching scenarios

How does Star match payments when the remittance is messy?

Real payments rarely map one-to-one. These are the scenarios Star handles every day for industrial AR teams.

One payment covering many invoices

A fabricator pays 38 invoices with one ACH and an Excel remittance. Star reads every line, matches each to its open item, checks the line totals against the deposit and applies the whole payment in one receipt. If one line does not reconcile, only that line goes to review — the other 37 post.

  • Line-by-line application, not a lump sum on account.
  • Totals verified against the bank deposit before posting.
  • Partial review: only the exception waits.

Partial payment against a large invoice

A contractor pays 60% of an invoice for the material released so far. Star applies the payment to the right lines, leaves the remainder open with the reason recorded, and — if you use payment requests — schedules a request for the balance when the next release ships.

  • Partial applications at invoice or line level.
  • Reason captured from the remittance note.
  • Balance stays visible to collections with context.

Missing, truncated or wrong invoice numbers

The remittance says "inv 2935" or lists only a PO. Star searches the customer's open items for combinations that equal the payment, weighs PO, reference, ship date and that customer's habits, and proposes the best match with a confidence score. Once confirmed, the mapping is remembered.

  • Amount-combination search across open items.
  • PO, BOL and customer-reference matching.
  • Customer-specific habits learned over time.

One wire across entities and currencies

A customer with accounts at your US and Canadian entities sends a single wire covering both. Star splits the payment by entity, converts at the rate on the payment date, applies each portion and posts one receipt per entity — with the FX difference booked to the right account.

  • Entity split from invoice ownership.
  • FX differences booked automatically.
  • Parent–child customer hierarchies supported.

Overpayments and duplicate payments

A customer pays an invoice twice, or pays more than is owed. Star detects the overpayment, checks for a likely duplicate, and applies the excess per your policy: hold on account, apply to the oldest open invoice, or queue a refund for approval.

  • Duplicate detection across channels.
  • Policy-driven handling of the excess.
  • Customer notified automatically if you choose.

Glossary

Accounts receivable terms, defined

AR automation
Software that automates the work between issuing an invoice and closing it: payment requests, remittance capture, cash application, deductions, collections and ERP posting.
Cash application
Matching each customer payment to the open invoices it pays and recording that application in the ledger.
Remittance advice
The document or data a customer sends to explain which invoices a payment covers and any amounts deducted.
Payment request
A secure pay-now link sent with one or more invoices or a statement, letting the customer pay by ACH or card without logging in.
Short pay
A payment for less than the invoiced amount, usually because the customer has taken a deduction.
Deduction
An amount a customer withholds from payment for a reason such as weight variance, freight, scrap return, pricing, rebate or a quality claim.
Weight variance
The difference between invoiced (often theoretical) weight and the weight the customer received, a common cause of short pays in metals.
Unapplied cash
Money received but not yet matched to specific invoices, which distorts aging, DSO and credit decisions.
DSO (days sales outstanding)
The average number of days it takes to collect payment after a sale; lower DSO means faster cash.
Lockbox
A bank service that receives customer cheques, deposits them and sends images and data files to the supplier.
EDI 820
The electronic data interchange transaction for payment order / remittance advice, used by large customers to describe what they paid.
Straight-through processing
The share of payments applied and posted automatically, without a person touching them.
Order-to-cash (O2C)
The full cycle from customer order and credit check through invoicing, payment and cash posting; AR automation covers the stages after the invoice.
Cash posting
Recording a customer receipt in the general ledger; often used interchangeably with cash application.
Auto-match (hit) rate
The percentage of payments matched to invoices automatically, without a person reviewing them.
Theoretical weight
Steel weight calculated from dimensions and grade rather than measured on a scale; differences from actual weight cause short pays.
CWT pricing
Pricing per hundredweight (100 lb), common for steel coil, sheet, plate and bar sold by service centers.
Retainage
A percentage of an invoice the customer withholds until a project milestone or completion, common in metal fabrication and manufacturing.
Chargeback (pharma)
The difference between a wholesaler's acquisition cost and the lower contract price a manufacturer agreed with a customer, claimed back from the manufacturer.
EDI 844 / EDI 849
EDI 844 is a wholesaler's chargeback claim (product transfer account adjustment); EDI 849 is the manufacturer's response accepting or rejecting each line.
Gross-to-net
The reduction from a pharmaceutical product's list price to net revenue after chargebacks, rebates, fees and returns.
GPO
Group purchasing organization: a buying group that negotiates contract prices with manufacturers on behalf of members.
